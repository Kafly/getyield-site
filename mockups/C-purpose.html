Yield only counts a win when the money actually lands.

That sounds like a small thing. It is the whole product. Anyone can show you a dashboard of what you might have saved. Yield holds the number back until a credit posts to your statement and matches a charge, and then it counts it once.

The same discipline runs everywhere else. Estimates stay labelled as estimates. Modeled value stays banded until a statement proves it. Position on your credit file is stated, never graded. When a month is clean, Yield says so rather than inventing a miss.

It also means Yield stops where you begin. It prepares the move and hands it to you. Scripts get copied, orders get placed, calls get made, refunds get asked for, by you.

Questions

The ones worth answering.

Does Yield actually do things for me, or just tell me?+
It prepares and stops. Yield reads your own data, works out the move, and puts it in front of you ready to go. Scripts get copied, orders get placed, calls get made, refunds get asked for, by you. Nothing is filed or purchased in your name. That is a deliberate line, not a missing feature.
What can Yield see?+
Card transactions, balances and limits through a read only Plaid connection, plus any receipt emails you choose to forward. Not your checking or savings accounts, not your card numbers, not your login. Receipt forwarding is off until you switch it on, and only reaches emails you send yourself.
Will this improve my credit score?+
Yield does not promise score outcomes and will not claim any. What it does is show you what the bureaus will see before they see it: your utilization, which cards report when, and what a payment before a close would change. Position is stated, never graded, and payments always stay in your hands.
How does it know a price actually dropped?+
It holds the exact item from your own receipt, by name, size and store, and reads prices from public product pages. When it shows you a drop it shows the evidence behind the number, including what you paid and what the price has done since.
What counts as money Yield got back?+
Only a credit that posted to your statement and matched a charge, counted once. Expected value, modeled value and anything still in flight stay in their own columns, clearly labelled.
How often will it interrupt me?+
Rarely. There is no daily digest and no streak. You hear from Yield when a credit is about to expire, when a tracked price drops inside a window, when one of your own bills creeps, and when a credit finally posts.
