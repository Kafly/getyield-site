The money leaves quietly. That is the design.

A premium card is a stack of obligations dressed as perks. Fourteen credits, six deadlines, four portals, each with its own reset rule. Nobody sends a reminder, because an unused credit is margin that never had to be paid out.

The same silence runs through the rest of it. Your internet bill steps up nineteen dollars at renewal, inside a line item you stopped reading eleven months ago. The item you bought on Tuesday is a quarter cheaper on Sunday, and the store will refund the difference if you ask inside its window. You will not notice. You are not supposed to.

None of this is carelessness. Every one of these systems is working exactly as intended, and the friction is the product. Yield is the party on your side of that arrangement.

Why it works

Nobody on the other side is being careless.

01

Your issuer will not remind you

A credit that expires unused is margin that never had to be paid out. There is no department whose job is to make sure you spend it. There is a department whose job is to make sure you keep paying the fee.

02

The refund window is quiet on purpose

Most large retailers will refund a price difference inside a set window. Almost nobody asks, because almost nobody is still watching the price of something they already own.

03

Your bills rise on a schedule

Introductory rates end. Renewal premiums step up. The increase arrives inside a line you stopped reading, and it compounds every year you do not challenge it.

The work

Five fights, one instrument.

Each one reads your own data. Each ends with a move you make, and a result Yield counts only once the money actually lands.

01 · Switchboard

Bills that could ride a benefit you already have.

You are paying full price for something one of your cards already covers. Yield finds those bills and shows what moving is worth, honestly, including the rewards you give up and the promotions you lose.

You make the change at the merchant. Yield watches your statement and confirms the credit only when it posts and matches the charge.

Nothing counted before it posts. A record, not a promise.
02 · Price Drop Refunds

You bought it. The price fell. The window is still open.

Most stores will refund the difference if you ask inside their window. The window is short, and it starts either the day you buy or the day it arrives.

Forward the order email. Yield holds the exact item, watches the price, and tells you the day it drops while you can still act. You make the ask. Yield never files anything as you.

Exact items, not guesses. Name, size and store.
03 · Watchdog

Quiet until there is a deal.

Paste a product link and Yield tracks that one item. Or forward your order emails, and Watchdog locks on to the things you actually buy again, by name, size and store.

It stays silent for weeks at a time. When a price moves in your favour on something you already buy, you hear about it, with the evidence behind the number.

Read from public pages and your own receipts. Never your inbox, never your location.
04 · Bill Creep

Your bill went up and nobody told you.

Insurance renewals, internet and cable, home security monitoring. These climb on a schedule, and the increase never arrives as an announcement.

Yield watches your own recurring bills and speaks only when one creeps, with what you paid before, what you pay now, and what the going rate looks like so you know what to ask for.

Creep in your own bills. Nothing generic, nothing borrowed.
05 · Point Max

Every purchase that sat on the wrong card.

Yield reads your last month end to end and finds the spend that earned one point where it could have earned four. It names the purchase, the card you used, the card you should have used, and what the gap cost.

When the month is clean, it tells you that instead of inventing a miss.

Questions

The ones worth answering.

Does Yield actually do things for me, or just tell me?+
It prepares and stops. Yield reads your own data, works out the move, and puts it in front of you ready to go. Scripts get copied, orders get placed, calls get made, refunds get asked for, by you. Nothing is filed or purchased in your name. That is a deliberate line, not a missing feature.
What can Yield see?+
Card transactions, balances and limits through a read only Plaid connection, plus any receipt emails you choose to forward. Not your checking or savings accounts, not your card numbers, not your login. Receipt forwarding is off until you switch it on, and only reaches emails you send yourself.
Will this improve my credit score?+
Yield does not promise score outcomes and will not claim any. What it does is show you what the bureaus will see before they see it: your utilization, which cards report when, and what a payment before a close would change. Position is stated, never graded, and payments always stay in your hands.
How does it know a price actually dropped?+
It holds the exact item from your own receipt, by name, size and store, and reads prices from public product pages. When it shows you a drop it shows the evidence behind the number, including what you paid and what the price has done since.
What counts as money Yield got back?+
Only a credit that posted to your statement and matched a charge, counted once. Expected value, modeled value and anything still in flight stay in their own columns, clearly labelled. Yield will not show a number it cannot stand behind.
How often will it interrupt me?+
Rarely. There is no daily digest and no streak. You hear from Yield when a credit is about to expire, when a tracked price drops inside a window, when one of your own bills creeps, and when a credit finally posts.
